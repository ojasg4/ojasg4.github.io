September 15, 2026 Where the protein lettering on the homepage comes from

The proteins spelling OJAS at the top of this site are real crystal structures of real proteins that happens to look like English letters. They come from Mark Howarth, who went through the Protein Data Bank manually to find a protein resembling every letter of the alphabet.

M. Howarth, “Say it with proteins: an alphabet of crystal structures,” Nature Structural & Molecular Biology 22, 349 (2015), https://doi.org/10.1038/nsmb.3011.

To understand what is the Protein Data Bank and why we can represent a "real protein crystal" with a bunch of loops and ribbons (which might feel silly at first), please see my previous blog post.

More information about the proteins representing the four letters in my name, pulled directly from Supplementary Table 1 of the paper:

Letter PDB Function Structure
O 2wcd Bacterial toxin Cytolysin A from Escherichia coli; 12 copies of a 3-helix bundle.
J 1b3u Intracellular signaling PR65/A subunit of Protein Phosphatase 2A from Homo sapiens; contains 15 HEAT motifs.
A 3ifz DNA topology DNA gyrase reaction core from Mycobacterium tuberculosis; a target of antibiotics.
S 2ot8 Nuclear import Transportin-1 from Homo sapiens recognizing a nuclear localization signal; contains HEAT repeats.

Structure details from the Supplementary Information of Howarth (2015), Supplementary Table 1.
