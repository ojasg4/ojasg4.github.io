D. Angioletti, M. Carli, M. Nobile, and V. Limongelli, “Deep Learning for Protein Modeling: From Single Structure to Bound Complex and Thermodynamic Ensemble, With a Focus on Architectural Design,” Wiley Interdisciplinary Reviews: Computational Molecular Science 16, no. 5 (2026): e70082, https://doi.org/10.1002/wcms.70082.

A brief synopsis

The review surveys how deep learning is used to model the shapes proteins take, and it organizes the field into three goals: predicting a single structure for one protein, predicting the structure of a bound complex, and generating the whole ensemble of conformations a protein visits. Its particular angle is architectural: it traces the design choices that recur across these models. Examples are whether a network has rotational and translational symmetry built in (SE(3) equivariance) or is left to learn it, whether evolutionary information comes from a multiple sequence alignment or from a protein language model, and whether the model returns one deterministic answer or samples from a generative process.

The authors’ assessment is that single-structure prediction is now relatively mature, while the other two goals are still open problems. Predicting bound complexes remains hard, and so does generating thermodynamic ensembles in which each conformation carries a reliable population weight, meaning how often the protein actually adopts it. They point to generative kinetics, models that capture how a protein moves between states over time, as a longer-term frontier.
