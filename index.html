About

I am a biomedical engineering senior at Georgia Tech with a minor in Chemistry & Biochemistry. I am working in computational structural biology with the goal of designing more effective drugs. I am also passionate about the translation of laboratory results into society and effective scientific communication, as science should be understandable by and work for everyone.

Research

NIH / NLM · Porter Lab

Fold-switching protein prediction

Fold-switching proteins adopt more than one stable fold, defying the current protein-folding paradigm. The total number of fold-switching proteins is likely significantly underreported and is of great interest to biologists and physicists. I worked on methods to estimate the total amount of fold-switching proteins in the human genome.

UCSF · Douglas Lab

DNA–protein hybrid nanostructures

DNA "origami" nanostructures hold significant potential in bio-engineering applications. Any functionality typically requires introducing a protein to the DNA nanostructure, which is currently not straightforward. I helped design a de-novo orthogonal protein-DNA binder that was then tested in vivo for immune system reprogramming.

Emory University · Quiroz Lab

Intrinsically disordered protein polymers

Intrinsically disordered proteins lack a defined structure and shift dynamically between several shapes. Intrinsically disordered protein polymers are an engineered class of molecules that go one step further and can form synthetic condensates, a localized separate compartment within a cell similar to an oil droplet in water. When functionalized into an organelle, these could achieve various synthetic biology applications such as augmented cells, but they currently face several implementation hurdles. I worked on establishing orthogonality, or independence, to human cellular processes.

Georgia Tech · Kane lab

Nanobody engineering

A nanobody is a class of antibodies inspired by the Camelid family (Camels, etc.) that could offer better performance and manufacturing metrics compared to normal antibodies. I was in charge of using directed evolution, an experimental process that mimics natural selection, to improve nanobody design for the COVID-19 XBB variant, contributing to a published study in npj Viruses.
